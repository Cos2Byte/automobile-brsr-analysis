
# Beyond Compliance: A Comparative BRSR-Based ESG Performance Analysis of India's Automobile Industry

## Project Overview

This project examines the environmental and social sustainability performance of four major Indian automobile manufacturers using disclosures from their Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reports (BRSR).

The study evaluates whether corporate sustainability disclosures can be used to understand actual performance outcomes rather than being treated merely as regulatory compliance documents.

The analysis covers four automobile manufacturers over three financial years, FY 2022–23 to FY 2024–25:

- Tata Motors Limited
- Mahindra & Mahindra Limited
- Maruti Suzuki India Limited
- Ashok Leyland Limited

The project applies an indicator-based, comparative approach to examine sustainability performance, identify differences across companies, and understand the trade-offs between environmental efficiency and social performance.

## Research Objectives

- Examine environmental and social performance using publicly disclosed BRSR data.
- Develop measurable sustainability indicators for comparative assessment.
- Analyse changes in selected sustainability indicators over three financial years.
- Compare relative performance across automobile manufacturers.
- Examine relationships between environmental efficiency and social outcomes.
- Identify company-specific strengths, gaps, and areas for improvement.

## Research Methodology

The study follows a comparative and indicator-based research design.

### 1. BRSR Report Review and Data Collection

Reviewed publicly available BRSR disclosures published by the selected companies in their annual reports for FY 2022–23, FY 2023–24, and FY 2024–25.

Relevant information was extracted from disclosures relating to energy consumption, greenhouse gas emissions, renewable energy, workforce safety, employee well-being, and gender-related performance.

### 2. Indicator Development

Developed sustainability performance indicators to facilitate comparison across companies and reporting years.

**Environmental Indicators**
- Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emission intensity
- Energy consumption intensity
- Renewable energy share

**Social Indicators**
- Employee well-being expenditure as a proportion of revenue
- Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR)
- Gender-related workforce indicators

Governance-related disclosures were considered qualitatively rather than incorporated as a separate quantitative score.

### 3. Data Organisation and Analysis

Consolidated the extracted information in Microsoft Excel, organised the data across companies and financial years, and calculated the selected performance indicators.

Only values disclosed in the companies' BRSR reports were used. No external estimates were introduced.

### 4. Normalisation and Peer Benchmarking

Applied Min-Max normalisation in Microsoft Excel to compare relative performance across companies.

For the primary comparative assessment (FY 2024–25), the best-performing company within the selected peer group was assigned a score of 100 and the worst-performing company a score of 0, with intermediate values scaled proportionally.

The resulting scores represent relative peer performance rather than absolute sustainability adequacy.

### 5. Data Visualisation and Comparative Assessment

Developed visualisations in Microsoft Excel to examine performance patterns and relationships, including:

- Comparative environmental and social performance matrix
- GHG emission intensity and renewable energy adoption scatter plot
- Three-year GHG emission intensity trend analysis
- Normalised ESG performance scorecard
- Overall ESG performance comparison across companies

### 6. Integrated ESG Interpretation

Interpreted the findings across environmental and social dimensions to identify performance differences, cross-dimensional trade-offs, and company-specific areas for improvement.

The study also developed industry-level and company-specific recommendations based on the comparative findings.

## Key Findings

The analysis highlights several important patterns across the selected automobile manufacturers:

- GHG emission intensity shows a declining trend across companies, although the pace and consistency of improvement differ.
- Higher renewable energy adoption does not consistently translate into lower GHG emission intensity.
- Operational efficiency and process-level improvements remain important factors in environmental performance.
- Workforce safety, employee well-being expenditure, and gender-related outcomes show uneven performance across companies.
- Strong environmental performance does not necessarily correspond with stronger social performance.
- The integrated ESG assessment highlights the importance of balanced sustainability strategies rather than focusing on individual indicators.

Overall, the findings suggest that BRSR disclosures can provide meaningful insights into corporate sustainability performance when systematically extracted, analysed, and compared.

## Tools and Technologies

- **Microsoft Excel:** Data organisation, indicator calculations, normalisation, comparative analysis, and visualisation.
- **BRSR Reports:** Primary sources for company-level sustainability disclosures.
- **Research and Analytical Methods:** Indicator development, peer benchmarking, trend analysis, and comparative ESG assessment.

## Limitations

- The analysis relies on publicly disclosed and self-reported BRSR data.
- The study covers four automobile manufacturers.
- Environmental analysis is restricted to Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions.
- Scope 3 emissions were excluded because of differences in reporting boundaries and data availability.
- Governance-related indicators were assessed qualitatively rather than quantitatively.
- Normalised scores reflect relative performance within the selected peer group and do not represent absolute sustainability standards.
